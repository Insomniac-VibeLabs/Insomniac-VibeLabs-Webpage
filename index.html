What's been built so far

Two projects so far, and they don't have a lot in common except the hour they were dreamed up. One is about keeping AI agents on a leash, the other is about money made from electricity. That's where the 3 a.m. thoughts went, not a declared focus.

  • two-key-concept

    Most agent guardrails ask an AI to police an AI. This one makes the AI's say-so not enough on its own.

    A small, open source take on AI agent security and authorization: two independent keys have to turn before an agent can act. One key is a policy engine with hard rules, the other is one or more LLM judges, and a single-use capability token only gets minted when both say yes. Every call lands in an encrypted audit ledger. Think of it as a guardrail that sits between an agent and its tools, handy against prompt injection and agents that get a little too creative. It's a prototype, Apache-2.0, and it doesn't scan payloads.

  • enerchain

    Mining spends electricity to make a coin. This one flips it: the coin is a record of the electricity you delivered.

    A research prototype for an electricity currency. A site gets two sealed meters, one at the solar array or generator and one where it connects to the grid. Each meter counts energy both ways and signs a record for every kilowatt-hour of net export, using post-quantum ML-DSA signatures. A battery that buys at night and sells at noon mints nothing. The repo has a Python devnet with wallet and ledger, chip RTL that simulates end to end, signer firmware, and a board design. It's all simulation and paperwork so far: nothing has been built or taped out. Apache-2.0 for the software and docs, CERN-OHL-S-2.0 for the hardware.

    The counting isn't left to a program. In the design, a small dedicated chip counts the meter's pulses and builds each record in fixed logic, and nothing outside it can write to its counters. A separate secure chip holds the key and signs only what that counter chip hands it, running a small published firmware image. So no app, inverter, or server gets to pick the number.

    And one meter isn't enough. Every site has two of them, each sealed, each with its own key: one at the generator says the power was made, and one at the grid connection says it left the site. Nothing gets credited unless both have signed, and then only up to the smaller of the two counts. A meter that's been fooled or tampered with can't mint more than its partner saw.
